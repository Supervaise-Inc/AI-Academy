## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Four new lesson figures.** `nested` (AI ⊃ machine learning ⊃ deep learning ⊃ generative AI), `sampling` (softmax with temperature plus top-k and top-p, with the formula's live numbers and a draw-100-times tally), `chain` (the chain rule: a sentence's probability as the product of next-word probabilities, with perplexity) and `logbars` (sizes on a log scale). Documented in `docs/LESSON_FORMAT.md` and checked by the validator, which now allows quizzes of up to 15 questions.
- **Colab notebooks for every lesson.** `notebooks/week-01.ipynb` … `week-20.ipynb` (20 notebooks, including Week 7). Each lesson's lab (or study-plan) section shows **Open in Colab** and **Download .ipynb** buttons; students run the notebook on Google's servers and save their own copy to Drive. Weeks 1–4, 6–9, 11–14 and 16–18 are lab notebooks; Weeks 5, 10, 15, 19 and 20 are practice notebooks with computed exercises. Every notebook has Your turn exercises, Check cells and worked answers. The built-in Week 7 lab's "Open Laboratory 3 notebook" button now opens its notebook.
- **Notebook toolchain.** Notebooks are written as percent-format Python in `notebooks/src/` and built with `npm run build:notebooks` (`tools/build-notebooks.mjs`, with structure checks). `npm run test:notebooks` compiles every cell; `python3 tools/test-notebooks.py --run week-NN` executes a notebook. Format and rules: `docs/NOTEBOOK_FORMAT.md`.
- **API:** `GET /api/notebooks/:slug` (Colab link and download path) and `GET /api/notebooks/:slug/download`; the lesson response also carries `notebook`. New settings `NOTEBOOKS_DIR`, `COLAB_REPO` (default `Supervaise-Inc/AI-Academy`) and `COLAB_BRANCH` (default `main`). One more backend test.
- **Week-driven Overview.** `/dashboard/:week` shows the selected week's term, learning activities, sessions and "next action". `/dashboard` still shows the current week (7).
- **`frontend/src/data/courseWeeks.js`**: one data file holding the term, activities, sessions, dates and hero for all 20 weeks. Weeks other than 7 use generated placeholder sessions (08:00 lecture, 10:30 lab, 14:00 consultation) and due dates (Saturday, 11:59 PM PHT); replace them with the real timetable.
- **Week overview** button on the Course Map, next to **Open lesson**.
- **`attention` lesson figure**: query, keys, values, dot products, softmax weights α and the weighted-sum output, computed live. Choose the query word and toggle √d scaling.
- **`equation` lesson figure**: a formula whose symbols can be selected for a plain-language meaning, with an optional worked example.
- Both figures are documented in `docs/LESSON_FORMAT.md` and checked by `tools/validate-lessons.mjs`.
- Week 8 lesson: an equation figure (scaled dot-product attention, worked example for "apple") and an attention diagram for "I am eating an apple".
- **Lab steps are easier to scan.** In lesson plans, numbered lab steps show a large green number badge (the numbering still restarts correctly after each code block) and a bold step title. Code boxes and exercises sit indented under their step.
- **Exercise callouts.** A paragraph that starts with `*Exercise N:*` in a lesson file becomes an amber callout with an "Exercise N" badge, so it stands out when skimming.

### Changed
- **Week 6 rewritten around language models, tokenization and word embeddings.** About 1,380 lines of lesson text, 35 figures of 16 types (eleven formula figures: chain rule of probability, perplexity, bigram, add-one smoothing and interpolation, vocabulary size and tokens per word, embedding lookup, cosine similarity), 15 quiz questions (11 calculations) and six rail sections (Language models, History, N-grams, Tokens, BPE, Embeddings, plus Laboratory). Covers the history from rules to n-grams to neural models, why modern LLMs use sub-word (BPE) tokens, and how ids become vectors.
- **Three new figure types:** `vocabtrade` (characters vs BPE pieces vs whole words: tokens and vocabulary size as merges grow, for any sentence you type), `vecsim` (dot product, lengths and cosine similarity of word vectors) and `calc` kind `ngram_space` (V^n possible n-grams against the text you have: the sparsity problem). The validator recomputes `vocabtrade` and `vecsim` checks. Very large values on the log-bar figure now print as powers of ten.
- **New Week 6 lab, "Imagine how a machine reads, then build the reader".** Seven parts: be a language model (counting, chain rule, perplexity, add-one smoothing), rules vs counts vs a tiny neural model, the limits of n-grams, real tokenizers (GPT-2 and BERT) and their quirks, a BPE tokenizer written from scratch and compared with GPT-2, word embeddings learned from a toy corpus (co-occurrence, PPMI, SVD and a skip-gram network), and designing a tokenizer for a community. Only small tokenizer files are downloaded. Rubric and deliverables included.
- **Week 6 notebook rebuilt** (149 code cells), run end to end on CPU in about 25 seconds (the optional MiniLM stretch cell is skipped).
- **Week 7 is now a Markdown lesson on language models and attention** (it was a hand-built page, which stays in the code as a fallback if `week-07.md` is removed). About 1,500 lines of lesson text, 42 figures of 14 types (fifteen formula figures: the RNN recurrence, gradient through time, the four LSTM gates and the cell update, scaled dot-product attention, hard attention, temperature, self-attention projections, the √d_k scaling), 15 quiz questions (9 calculations) and six rail sections (Sequences, RNN, LSTM, Attention, Hard vs soft, Self-attention, plus Laboratory). It explains why RNNs and LSTMs struggle with long sequences, why attention replaced them, and the difference between hard and soft attention.
- **Four new figure types:** `seqmemory` (how much of step t still reaches the end for an RNN, an LSTM and attention), `lstmcell` (one LSTM step with its four gates), `hardsoft` (hard attention picks one position, soft attention blends; shows why only soft is trainable) and `calc` kind `attn_cost` (the n × n cost of attention). The validator recomputes their checks.
- **New Week 7 lab, "Imagine a model that remembers, then give it attention"** (GA Lab 3, Part 1). Seven parts built around a memory task (the label is the first symbol of a long random sequence): sequences and fixed windows, an RNN from scratch, gradients through time, an LSTM cell by hand, attention by hand (including why we divide by √d_k and the n² cost), hard versus soft attention, and a self-attention layer. Rubric and deliverables included.
- **Week 7 notebook rebuilt** (130 code cells), run end to end on CPU in about 30 seconds. Honest finding reported in the lab: with these settings the LSTM does not remember longer than the RNN, while attention with position embeddings solves every length tested.
- **Week 8 rewritten around the Transformer block and autoregressive training.** About 1,700 lines of lesson text, 48 figures of 16 types (sixteen formula figures: sinusoidal positional encoding and its rotation property, multi-head attention shapes and parameters, the feed-forward network, residual connections, layer norm, the training loss, the masked softmax, the 12·L·d² rule), 15 quiz questions (11 calculations) and six rail sections (Big picture, Positions, Multi-head, FFN and norm, Autoregressive, Putting together, plus Laboratory). Covers the parts of a Transformer block, why positional encoding is needed and how to compute the sinusoidal version, and how next-token training works with shifted targets and the causal mask.
- **Three new figure types:** `tfblock` (a parameter and shape table for a decoder-only Transformer with editable sizes; the GPT-2 small setting gives 124,439,808), `causalmask` (switch the mask on and off and read the attention weights) and `shiftloss` (inputs, targets shifted by one, and the loss at every position, with mean loss and perplexity). The validator recomputes their checks.
- **New Week 8 lab, "Imagine the Transformer, then build it"** (GA Lab 3, Part 2). Seven parts: sinusoidal positions by hand, multi-head attention with the (batch, heads, seq, d_head) shapes, the causal mask and shifted targets, feed-forward, residuals and layer norm, assembling a tiny GPT and counting its parameters, training it with ablations (no mask, no positions, heads and depth, learning rate), and scaling on paper. Rubric and deliverables included.
- **Week 8 notebook rebuilt** (152 code cells), run end to end on CPU in about 30 seconds. Honest findings reported in the lab: without the causal mask the training loss drops suspiciously low but the generated text is garbage; positional encoding made no difference on the simple repeated sentence but did on a position-dependent pattern; heads and layers changed the loss by less than 0.01 on this tiny text.

The page explains the exam (marked out of 100: Part A written exam 40 points in 60 minutes, Part B practical exam 60 points in 120 minutes, pass 70), a coverage blueprint, rules and integrity, then reviews Weeks 6–9 as just rewritten (50 figures of 23 types, 24 formula figures with new worked numbers, three worked practice problems per week), a one-page formula sheet and a 4-day revision plan.

- **Part B, practical exam:** five timed tasks in a notebook (tokens and language models, sequences and attention, the Transformer and autoregressive training, LLM families, scaling and prompting, and a written synthesis) with new numbers. - **Week 9 rewritten around LLM families, scaling laws and prompt engineering.** About 1,500 lines of lesson text, 51 figures of 19 types (fourteen formula figures: the masked and causal losses, MoE routing and the balance loss, power laws, C ≈ 6ND and the Chinchilla rule, exact match p^L, majority-vote and standard-error numbers), 15 quiz questions (10 calculations) and six rail sections (Families, MoE, Scaling laws, Chinchilla, Emergence, Prompting, plus Laboratory). Compares encoder-only (BERT), decoder-only (GPT), encoder-decoder (T5) and mixture-of-experts models, explains scaling laws, compute-optimal training and emergent abilities (with the debate about metrics), and prepares for the Prompt Lab.
